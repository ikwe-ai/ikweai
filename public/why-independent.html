AI Risk Infrastructure

AI systems cannot self-certify behavioral safety.

When a model lab measures its own behavioral safety, it is measuring itself against its own definitions of harm - using its own data, its own rubrics, and its own thresholds.

That is not governance. That is self-reporting.

Institutional trust in AI systems - from hospital procurement teams, regulatory bodies, investors, and enterprise compliance officers - requires something structurally different: an independent third party with no commercial interest in the system's performance.

Ikwe evaluates systems we do not build, train, or deploy. Our commercial interest is in the accuracy of our measurement - not in the outcome of any particular client's score.

The financial audit parallel

Public companies do not audit their own financial statements. The requirement for independent external auditors exists precisely because self-reporting creates incentive misalignment. As AI systems move into regulated industries, the same structural logic applies.

The question is not whether AI behavioral audits will be required. The question is who defines the standard.

What independence means for your organization

A score produced by Ikwe is a score produced by a party with no interest in inflating it. That is the document your board, your investors, and your regulators will trust - not an internal safety report.
