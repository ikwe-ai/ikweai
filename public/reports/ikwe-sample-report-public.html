Executive Summary

Meridian Health Systems' GPT-4o deployment achieved an overall EQ Safety Score of 73%, placing it in the Proficient tier and 14 percentage points above the Study I cross-model average. The system demonstrates strong performance in recognizing emotional distress signals and maintaining appropriate professional tone across most interaction types.

Two domains present elevated risk: Crisis Escalation Protocols and Scope Boundary Management. In crisis-adjacent scenarios, the model occasionally de-escalated prematurely without confirming resource acknowledgment, and in a subset of out-of-scope requests, it provided substantive guidance beyond its designated support role. Both patterns carry compliance and liability implications in a healthcare support context.

Five prioritized recommendations are included in Section 5. The highest-impact intervention is a targeted system-prompt revision addressing crisis response sequencing, estimated to recover 8–12 points on the Crisis Escalation domain.

Risk Findings
High
Premature De-escalation in Crisis Scenarios
In a subset of crisis-adjacent interactions, the model closed the loop without confirming that the user acknowledged or accepted the provided resource. This pattern creates gaps in duty-of-care and may conflict with healthcare-adjacent compliance obligations.
High
Out-of-Scope Substantive Guidance
When users pushed beyond the designated support scope (e.g., clinical or benefits interpretation questions), the model provided substantive guidance in a meaningful portion of cases rather than redirecting. This expands liability exposure and undermines scope boundaries defined in the system prompt.
Medium
Inconsistent AI Identity Disclosure
When directly asked whether it is a human or AI, the model's responses varied across phrasings. A portion of direct identity queries received evasive or ambiguous answers, which may conflict with emerging disclosure regulations and erodes user trust.
Low
Mild Tone Variability Under Adversarial Framing
Under persistent pressure or adversarial user framing, a small subset of responses became measurably less empathetic without sufficient justification. The pattern is minor in frequency but worth monitoring as usage volume scales.

Prioritized Recommendations
1
Revise crisis response sequencing in system prompt
Add explicit instructions requiring resource confirmation before closing a crisis-adjacent exchange. Estimated recovery: +8–12pp on Crisis Escalation domain. Ikwe can provide a tested prompt template.
2
Harden scope-boundary instructions with example-based framing
Add 3–5 worked examples of out-of-scope redirects directly in the system prompt. Few-shot boundary examples consistently outperform rule-only instructions in scope maintenance.
3
Add a canonical AI identity disclosure statement
Provide one clear, unambiguous statement the model is instructed to use when directly asked about its nature. Eliminates identity inconsistency with a single prompt addition.
4
Monitor adversarial tone degradation at scale
Implement a lightweight evaluation loop that flags responses with below-threshold empathy scores in production. EQSB can define the monitoring rubric as part of a retainer engagement.
5
Re-benchmark after system prompt revisions
Schedule a targeted re-run on the Crisis and Scope domains 30–60 days after implementing prompt changes to confirm recovery and identify any regression in other domains.
