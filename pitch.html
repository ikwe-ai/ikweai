1 · Problem & Market Shift

AI is already in human‑sensitive conversations. The behavioral safety stack is missing.

Why this matters now

Frontier models are quickly being deployed into support, care, coaching, and education — all domains where emotional harm, escalation failures, or boundary violations are unacceptable.

  • Technical safety ≠ behavioral safety. Red‑teaming, jailbreak filters, and content safety APIs catch obvious violations, not subtle relational harm.
  • Regulators and boards lack behavioral evidence. They see model cards and latency charts, not how a system actually treats a distressed user at 2 AM.
  • Incidents are discovered via headlines. Most behavioral failures surface after users are already harmed and screenshots are circulating on social media.

Convergence of pressure

  • Policy: EU AI Act, emerging US frameworks, and sector regulators are all starting to require context‑specific evidence of safety, not just technical attestations.
  • Market: Enterprises are under pressure to deploy AI now — but their lawyers, ethics teams, and PR leads want proof they are not creating headline risk.
  • Behavioral frontier: Models are becoming emotionally expressive by default. Without a dedicated behavioral safety layer, "emotional intelligence" can amplify harm.

4 · Proof & Benchmarks

We turn vague "trust" claims into legible, benchmarked behavioral evidence.

Evidence instead of vibes

Ikwe's EQ Safety Benchmark (EQSB) runs multi‑scenario, multi‑domain evaluations that look at how conversational systems actually behave across:

  • Emotional recognition & empathy: whether the system detects distress, validates emotion, and maintains supportive tone over time.
  • Crisis escalation protocols: whether the system safely hands off to humans or resources when conversations cross critical thresholds.
  • Scope boundaries: whether the system respects deployment scope instead of offering unqualified medical, financial, or legal advice.
  • Cultural and contextual sensitivity: whether responses adapt appropriately across identity, culture, and power dynamics.

Output is not just a score — it's a safety record:

  • Headline EQSB score & tier plus domain‑level breakdowns that boards and regulators can read.
  • Human‑readable risk findings that describe failure modes in plain language, with examples and impact framing.
  • Targeted recommendations for system‑prompt, data, and deployment changes tied to predicted safety gains.
  • Artifacts you can share with customers, partners, and regulators as proof of due diligence and ongoing monitoring.
5 · Model & Go‑To‑Market

Start with benchmarks, land as ongoing safety infrastructure.

Motion: benchmark → monitor → trust layer

Phase 1 — Benchmark Engagements

High‑trust, scoped engagements with teams already deploying human‑facing AI (support, care, education, fintech, gov). Output: EQSB report, risk findings, and roadmap.

These initial benchmarks establish the standard, build reference customers, and define the evaluation corpus for each vertical.

Phase 2 — Live Monitoring Retainers

Embed Ikwe as the behavioral safety layer for critical deployments. Light integration, ongoing scenario refresh, and anomaly monitoring tied to real conversations.

Revenue becomes recurring and tied to conversation volume, number of deployments, and regulatory / governance scope.

Phase 3 — Trust Infrastructure & Network Effects

Standardized EQSB scores and artifacts can become a de facto trust mark — analogous to security certifications or credit ratings, but for behavioral safety.

Over time, platforms, regulators, and buyers can start to require or prefer deployments with verifiable EQSB‑style safety records.
