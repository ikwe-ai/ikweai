The EQ Safety Benchmark

Evidence-based measurement of behavioral safety for AI.

Ikwe scores the whole conversation, not single replies, against the standard a licensed human in the same role is already held to. The judgment is clinical. Six disciplines settled what helps and what harms, over decades of practice. What did not exist was a way to check whether an AI does it. That is what we built, and the result goes on the record.

Why the seat is empty

Two fields. Neither one can answer this alone.

Clinicians know what helps

Six disciplines settled what good care looks like over decades, and they enforce it with licences, supervision and duty of care. What they cannot do is read a million conversations or inspect a model.

Engineers know how to check

Benchmarks, evals, red teams, all running at scale. What they do not have is a criterion for what good care looks like in the sixth turn of a hard conversation.

The harm lives in between

Whether an AI was safe for the person in front of it is a clinical question that has to be answered at engineering scale. It takes both fields, and almost nobody has both. The EQSB is what happens when you put them together.

We did not invent the ethics. Six clinical disciplines did, over decades of practice. The EQSB is the instrument that applies them, and Ikwe is the one running it, with nothing riding on the answer.

The bar

The standard already exists. It was just never applied to the machine.

Every one of the eight behavioral dimensions in the EQSB is a behavior a licensed human is already trained and required to perform.

Where you are

Three situations. Whichever one is yours, that is your answer.

One method, the EQSB, applied three ways. These are not stages and there is no bottom rung. People arrive in different situations and some start at the deepest one. Monitoring contains Assessment points, so it does not sit above it.

Assessment · one-time · about five business days
"I have to prove something right now."
  • A board, a carrier or a customer has asked what your AI does in a hard conversation, and you do not have an answer on paper.
  • A deal is sitting on a safety review.
  • A statutory deadline is in front of you and you have measured nothing.
  • You want to know where you stand before someone else tells you.

What it is. Your system against our standard scenario set, scored across whole conversations rather than single replies, documented and time-stamped. Every failure comes back with the transcript and the turn it happened on.

Audit · one-time · scoped
"A generic scenario set cannot answer my question."
  • Your users are specific, and so are the ways a conversation with them goes wrong.
  • You have a named obligation and the assessment needs to be scoped to it.
  • You already know roughly where you are weak, and you need it evidenced rather than discovered.
  • You have made fixes and you need the re-score to mean something.

What it is. Scenarios built for your domain and your users, whole conversations scored across the arc, written up for a board, a carrier or a regulator. Includes a re-score after you make changes. We never fix what we score.

Monitoring · subscription · continuous
"I do not know yet what I will be asked to prove."
  • You know a question is coming and you cannot predict its shape.
  • Your model changes. A review from March does not describe what shipped in August.
  • You need to go backwards through the data, not just take a reading today.
  • Oregon will ask you for annual crisis-referral counts, and a count is a history.

What it is. Continuous scoring of your live, anonymized traffic, with Assessment points built into the subscription. The evidence stack, building while nothing is on fire.

A baseline is retrospective. You cannot build one after you need it. Start in December and you have a data point. Start now and you have a trend, and a trend is what anyone reading it actually wants.

How we work with your system. We score anonymized inputs and outputs over a simple API. No codebase, no system prompt, no user identities. Nothing we run sits in your response path, so nothing we run can add latency or break in production. You own every record we produce, and nothing is published or shared without you.
The preventable window

Drift is visible before harm is.

That gap is the whole reason to measure continuously rather than once.

Harm does not arrive in one reply. It builds across a conversation while every single message passes. Measurement is what makes the window visible. What you do inside it is yours, because you hold the response path and you decide what changes.

The clock

The law is here. It does not say how to prove anything.

Fourteen states passed AI chatbot laws in 2026. California and New York are in force today. Washington, Oregon, Colorado, Georgia and Rhode Island go live on January 1, 2027, and Iowa, Nebraska and Idaho follow that July. Three of them let a user sue directly, without waiting for a regulator.

California requires operators to use evidence-based methods for measuring suicidal ideation. Oregon will require you to publish how many crisis referrals you issued. Those are measurement obligations. Measurement is an instrumentation problem, and your legal team cannot close it with a policy.

Not one of these laws defines the method. That means every operator in scope is choosing a method right now, by default, and will have to defend that choice later.

Every framework assumes a behavioral standard exists. Ikwe is building the one they will point to.

The choice

The alternative to measuring these tools is banning them.

People are already going to conversational AI at two in the morning, and for a lot of them it is the only thing awake. Many of them are helped by it.

Everything else a person turns to in distress is held to a standard. The hotline has protocols. The clinic has a licensing board. The counselor has supervision and a duty of care. None of that exists because anyone assumed they would cause harm. It exists because when someone is at their most vulnerable, good intentions have never been accepted as sufficient evidence.

This is where people are going now. Holding it to the same bar is the condition for keeping it.

Behavioral health was never one-size-fits-all, and that is exactly why a checkbox cannot answer this. The same sentence can be right in the first exchange and harmful in the sixth, depending on what the system has already been told. That is a measurement problem. Measurement is what lets these tools stay available instead of being legislated away from the people who need them.

The tools help. Measurement is how we keep them.
